# ShipBrief visual direction round

## Grounded candidate list

1. Annotated editorial proof sheet — familiar to people who review product copy against source material; supports sentence-to-commit evidence.
2. Split-flap departures board — familiar to builders watching queues and job states; makes new pushes and review status scan quickly.
3. Technical release field journal — turns commit notes into a traceable record, but is close to the proof-sheet idea.
4. Flight dispatch progress strips — gives each push a clear route from intake, through evidence, to human review; assigned by the concept roll.
5. Modular typographic grid specimen — appeals to design-minded builders and makes relationships explicit, but is less tied to the push ritual.
6. Audio mixing console — familiar control surface for tuning a message, but the metaphor maps poorly to source evidence.
7. Airport wayfinding route map — makes the next decision obvious, but can hide detailed evidence behind a simplified path.

The list uses paper/editorial, mechanical/electronic, and graphic/wayfinding material families. The incumbent generic dark developer console is the predictable opposite and is kept out of the candidate list.

## Roll adjudication

The assigned direction is Flight Dispatch Progress Strips. Competing roll challengers were fused with ShipBrief's real content before judgment.

| Challenger | Verdict | Audience identification | Product clarity | Discipline kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Split-Flap Concourse | Competitive | Builders recognize queued jobs and live states. | Makes the incoming queue clear, with evidence needing a second region. | Fixed-cell state labels and a restrained mechanical transition. |
| Crouwel Grid Specimen | Competitive | Strong with design-minded builders, narrower for general app builders. | Excellent at aligning queue, evidence, and draft. | A strict construction grid and high typographic precision. |
| Yellow Wayfinding | Competitive | Clear metaphor for people moving work through a pipeline. | The next human decision is very clear; evidence detail needs room. | A single amber active-step signal and an explicit route. |
| Dark Developer Console | Declined | Familiar to builders but repeats the incumbent generic look. | Dense, but status density does not make evidence relationships clear. | Compact semantic labels and information density. |
| Pop Culture Shelf Catalog | Declined | Low connection to the code-to-review scene. | Shelf ranking obscures evidence and approval. | Scannable hierarchy for incoming pushes. |
| Labanotation Score | Declined | The notation language is unfamiliar to the audience. | Its timeline does not explain code evidence. | A clear chronological progression. |

Raises transferred into the assigned direction are named on the direction card. The user requested a more distinctive, Shopify-quality visual result with both dimensional tactile surfaces and actual 3D objects and motion; this is treated as a pinned aesthetic across all candidate comps.
